Golden Bear Cannabis Co.

A vertically integrated cannabis operator in California

What the business does, how product and money move through it, what it records along the way, and the questions it needs answered in order to run.

1 · About this document

Golden Bear Cannabis Co. is a fictional company. It was constructed to exercise a realistic set of commercial, operational and regulatory problems. The names, registration details, ownership and people are invented.

The figures are not arbitrary: every number here is computed from a single coherent three-year dataset, so the totals reconcile with one another. Treat them as a worked example, not as the record of a real business.

This brief describes the company as it operates. It covers what the business does, how product and money move through it, what it records along the way, and which questions it needs to answer in order to run. It deliberately says nothing about how any of that is implemented.

It is written for someone joining the business, advising it, or evaluating it — the kind of thing you would want to have read before your first meeting.

6 · What the company does

Vertical integration means the company owns the product from seed to sale. In practice that is six distinct businesses that happen to share a balance sheet.

Cultivation

Six mixed-light rooms in Salinas, running continuous staggered cycles rather than a single seasonal harvest. A room is planted, vegetated, flowered and cut as a unit, and the cut becomes a harvest batch — a single cultivar, typically around 164 plants, tracked from then on as one lot. Roughly 420 batches over three years.

Manufacturing

Dried flower is turned into vapes, edibles, prerolls and concentrates. A production run draws material from several harvest batches at once — four on average — because extraction yields and terpene profiles are blended to hit a consistent product spec. This is ordinary practice and commercially sensible. It is also the single most consequential fact in this document, for reasons set out in section 7.

Distribution

The legal chokepoint. Every movement between sites, and every delivery to a third-party dispensary, travels on a manifest issued under the distribution licence. Nothing moves without one.

Retail

Four dispensaries and a delivery operation. 416,795 tickets over three years, averaging 2.6 lines each. Adult-use dominates; a small medical cohort carries different tax treatment. Staff — budtenders — advise, sell, and apply discounts, which makes them a meaningful driver of margin as well as of revenue.

Wholesale

The company's own brand sold into about 180 independent dispensaries. Account managers hold territories, work against a published price book, and raise purchase orders. Around 4,600 orders over three years, of which roughly three-quarters close.

Merchandise

Hoodies, hats, grinders, glass, rolling trays. It never touches the plant, which means it can be sold online, shipped anywhere, and paid for by card — none of which is true of the rest of the business.

7 · How product moves

Every plant and every package carries a state-issued tag, and every movement is manifested. In principle that gives complete traceability from a sold gram back to the plants it grew on. In practice the chain survives intact for one product family and breaks for the other.

The consequence is commercial, not clerical. "Which cultivars earn us the most?" is a straightforward question for flower and prerolls — 50% of retail revenue — and an unanswerable one for vapes, edibles and concentrates, which are the other half. It becomes answerable only once the business decides how to apportion a blended product's revenue across its inputs: by input weight, by input cost, or by potency contribution. That is a commercial judgement, and it has to be made deliberately rather than assumed by whoever writes the report.

A second complication: packages are split. A bulk package is broken down for different stores, and those children are sometimes split again — 2,041 of 13,381 packages are children of another. Tracing a sale to its origin means walking back up a chain of unknown depth.

Compliance testing gates everything: a batch cannot be sold until a certificate of analysis passes. The current pass rate is 96.2%. A failure means remediation or destruction, and it lands after the cultivation cost has already been incurred.

8 · How money moves

A single retail sale carries four separate tax layers, levied by three different authorities, at rates that differ by city. What the customer pays and what the company keeps are separated by a stack that has to be understood before any revenue figure means anything.

Local cannabis tax is the most variable element. Los Angeles levies 10%, Oakland 5%, Sacramento 4%, San Francisco 2.5% — so the same basket costs measurably different amounts in four shops belonging to the same company, and store-to-store comparisons of anything tax-inclusive are meaningless without adjustment.

Rates also change by statute, mid-year. A step in an effective tax rate is usually a change in the law rather than an error in the books, and the two have to be distinguishable.

Cash and collection

Retail is largely cash, with the handling and security costs that implies. Wholesale is invoiced on terms and paid, on average, 59 days after invoice — well past the stated terms of 14 to 60 days. Collections are therefore a standing management concern rather than a back-office task, and the gap between recognised wholesale revenue and received wholesale cash is material.

Where the systems meet

Two identifiers travel reliably across this estate and one does not. Product is solved: a SKU in the catalogue is the same SKU in the point of sale, the merchandise store and the price book. Compliance is solved: a package tag issued by the state is quoted by the point of sale when the package is sold and by the CRM when it is shipped, which is what makes traceability real.

The person is not solved. The counter knows a phone number, the merchandise store knows an email address, the marketing platform knows an email address, and the social channel knows nothing at all. There is no customer identifier that spans them, and there is no system whose job it is to hold one.

The track-and-trace record is the most complete and the least commercially legible: it knows the weight of every gram and the price of nothing. The point of sale is the reverse. Neither knows anything about the other beyond a package tag — which turns out to be enough for compliance, and not nearly enough for a question about strains and margin.

14 · Questions the business needs answered

Grouped by who asks them. Most are routine. A few, marked, do not have a single honest answer — see section 15.

Chief Executive and board
  • What is total revenue this quarter, split retail, wholesale and merchandise — and is that number comparable to last quarter? (contested)
  • Which of the three streams is actually earning, after tax and cost of goods?
  • How much of the company's revenue depends on the four largest wholesale accounts?
  • Are we carrying too much inventory, and where?
  • Which stores are outperforming their market, as opposed to their city's tax rate?
Chief Financial Officer
  • What is the effective tax rate by store and by month, and did any change come from the law rather than from us?
  • What is margin by product after all taxes — and does that ranking change when quoted tax-inclusive? (contested)
  • What is the cost per gram produced, by batch, landed?
  • What is outstanding wholesale receivable, aged, by account?
  • How much cash is tied up in stock that has not moved in 90 days?
Head of retail
  • Revenue and basket size per store per day; which days and hours need staffing?
  • Which budtenders discount hardest, and does it earn anything back in basket size?
  • What is the category mix by store, and where is it drifting?
  • How do delivery, pickup and walk-in compare once fulfilment cost is counted?
  • What share of customers return within 30, 60 and 90 days? (contested)
Head of wholesale
  • How many accounts ordered in the last 90 days, and which have gone quiet?
  • What is the reorder rate and the typical gap between orders, by account tier?
  • How far below the price book are we actually selling, by representative?
  • Which accounts are slow payers, and what is the exposure?
  • How many visits does an order take, and is that improving?
Head of cultivation and production
  • Yield per plant and per square foot, by cultivar and by room.
  • Which cultivars fail testing most often, and what does a failure cost?
  • What is the spread of potency within a cultivar, and is it tightening?
  • How long from cut to shelf, and where is the time going?
  • Which cultivars are worth the room they occupy? (contested)
Marketing
  • Did a given campaign move revenue — and how much of the answer is measurable? (contested)
  • What does a loyalty sign-up cost, by source?
  • How many merchandise buyers also shop in the dispensaries? (contested)
  • Which posts actually drive profile visits and sign-ups, as opposed to reach?
  • Are creator collaborations returning anything traceable?
Compliance
  • Does tagged inventory reconcile against physical stock, by site?
  • Which packages failed testing, and where did every unit of that batch end up?
  • Which licences renew in the next 120 days?
  • Are any manifests outstanding or unreceived?

15 · Why some answers are hard

Five structural features of this business make certain questions resistant. None is a failure of record-keeping — the records are unusually complete. They are properties of the trade itself.

One word, six meanings

"Revenue" can mean list price before discount, the figure after discount, the pre-tax subtotal on the ticket, what the customer handed over, the value of a wholesale purchase order, or merchandise takings. Four of those are in daily use inside the retail business alone. Two people can report the same week correctly and differ by 33%.

Facts are recorded at different sizes

A ticket is one size. A line on that ticket is another. A package is another. A harvest batch is another. A plant is another again. Counting something recorded at one size while grouping by something recorded at another is the easiest way in this business to produce a confident, plausible and badly wrong number.

Most revenue cannot be attributed to marketing

3.9% of counter revenue carries any link to a campaign. The rest is genuinely unattributable, and no amount of analysis recovers it. Reporting a single campaign return figure would mean silently treating the 96% as though it did not exist.

Stock is a reading, not a total

Units on hand at a store is a measurement taken at a moment. Thirteen weekly readings added together count the same box thirteen times. Stock can be averaged, or taken at a point, but it cannot be summed across time — and it can be summed across stores, which makes the mistake easy to make.

Blended products have no single cultivar

As set out in section 7. Half of retail revenue comes from products whose cultivar attribution requires a commercial decision that has not yet been made.

16 · Risks and constraints

Federal position

Cannabis remains federally controlled. The practical consequence for a profitable operator is section 280E of the federal tax code, which disallows ordinary business deductions and permits only cost of goods sold. The effective federal rate on a cannabis business is therefore far above the statutory one, and the classification of a cost as production rather than overhead has real money attached to it. The company retains a tax specialist on the board for this reason alone.

Banking

Access to conventional banking is limited and expensive, and can be withdrawn. The business is substantially cash-handling, with the security, insurance and reconciliation burden that follows. It also narrows the investor pool and raises the cost of capital.

Advertising

The restrictions described in section 10 are not a marketing preference but a legal constraint, and they will not relax on any timetable the company controls.

Licence renewal

All eight licences renew within an 18-month window beginning January 2027. Renewal is routine but not automatic, and a lapse at the distribution licence would halt every product movement in the company — including between its own sites.

Recall exposure

A failed test after distribution triggers a recall traced by package tag. Split packages make the affected set larger and slower to establish than the original batch would suggest.

Concentration

180 wholesale accounts sounds diversified, but order value is heavily skewed toward the key tier, and the 59-day average collection period means a single large account failing would be felt in cash before it was felt in revenue.

17 · Glossary

Adult-use / medical
The two customer categories. They carry different tax treatment and different purchase limits.
Batch
A harvest lot: one cut, one cultivar, tracked as a unit from the moment it comes down.
Budtender
Retail floor staff. Advises, sells, and applies discretionary discount.
Certificate of analysis
The laboratory report that gates a batch for sale. Records potency, terpenes and contaminant screens.
Cultivar
A named strain — Blue Dream, OG Kush. Drives both price and customer preference.
Distributor
The licence class permitted to move product between licensed premises. A legal role, not a logistics contractor.
Excise tax
The state cannabis tax, levied at retail and collected from the consumer.
Manifest
The document accompanying every movement of product between premises. No manifest, no movement.
Out-the-door price
What the customer actually pays, all taxes included. What the shop floor quotes.
Package
A tagged, saleable unit. May be split into child packages, which inherit the lineage.
Production run
One manufacturing operation, typically drawing on several harvest batches at once.
Section 280E
The federal provision disallowing ordinary deductions for businesses trafficking in controlled substances.
Storefront / non-storefront
Retail with premises, versus delivery-only retail. Separate licence classes.
Track and trace
The state-mandated system tagging every plant and package from propagation to sale.
